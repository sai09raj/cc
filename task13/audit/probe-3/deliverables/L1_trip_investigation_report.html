230 kV Line L1 – Substation A breaker trip of 14 September 2026, 14:22:07

Protection engineering investigation report. Case file L1_trip_case_r3_2026-09-14. All numbers in this report are outputs of the replay program l1_trip_replay.py (console log replay_console_log.txt, values in l1_trip_results.json). Relay sample k = n − 1 (n = first column of the .dat files); time = 14:22:07.3450 + k/1920 s, printed truncated to 0.1 ms as in the relays' SER.

Conclusion. The disturbance was a phase B–C fault (no ground) on line L2, 6.48 km from Substation B (patrol: tower 18, 6.50 km), beginning at relay sample k = 320 (14:22:07.5116). L2 was correctly cleared at both ends. The L1 trip at Substation A was a POTT scheme misoperation caused by relay B (L1-21 at Substation B) echoing relay A's permissive signal for a reverse fault.
Root cause: when the BF-50 breaker-failure relay of bay L1 at Substation B was replaced on 2025-02-12 (cable schedule bay L1, revision C), its current inputs were wired in parallel with the L1-21 relay current inputs (both fed from TS-L1:2/4/6, both returned to TB-L1:10) instead of in series as required by AC schematic B-E-3301. The CT secondary current divides in inverse proportion to the input burdens (relay 0.04 Ω, BF-50 0.06 Ω), so relay B measures only 60 % of the true current and sees every impedance 1/0.60 = 1.67 times too large. For this reverse, resistive L2 fault relay B's BC-loop impedance (−1.225 − j1.124 Ω sec) fell outside its reverse zone 3 (Z3P = 1.74 Ω), so neither Z3R nor the EBLK timer blocked the weak-infeed echo enabled at B on 2026-05-26 (settings R5). Relay B echoed relay A's zone 2 KEY, and relay A tripped by POTT.
Without the wiring error (series connection, full current), the replay shows relay B's Z3R asserting at k = 350, 13 samples before the permissive arrives (k = 363); the echo is blocked, relay A keys but never receives RX, and neither L1 relay trips.
Corrective action: re-terminate the bay L1 CT circuit at Substation B in series per B-E-3301 and re-commission it; set ECHO = N at relay B as an interim measure until the circuit is corrected; after correction restore ECHO = Y with the R5 values unchanged (Z3P = 1.74 Ω sec, EDPU 32, EDUR 64, EBLK 64). No change at relay A.

1.1 Replay program

The program implements the manual literally and evaluates every sample in the specified order: (1) full-cycle Fourier phasor X(k) = (√2/32)·Σ x[k−i]·e−j2π(k−i)/32 for k ≥ 31; (2) loops AB, BC, CA with V = VX − VY, I = IX − IY, evaluated only for |I| ≥ 50PP, Z = V/I; forward mho |Z − (R/2)ejT| ≤ R/2 for Z1/Z2, reverse mho |Z + (R/2)ejT| ≤ R/2 for Z3R; (3) RX(k) = remote KEY(k − 12); (4) echo: AND(/Z1, /Z2, /Z3R, /EBLK, RX) → EDPU pickup (asserts on the 32nd consecutive sample) → EDUR one-shot (64 samples, not retriggerable), EBLK = dropout timer holding 64 samples after Z3R drops; (5) KEY = Z2 + ECHO (ECHO only when ECHO = Y); (6) TRIP = latch(Z1 + Z2·RX); 52A opens 96 samples after TRIP; fault locator d = Xprimary/0.48 at the trip sample. The two ends of a line are simulated in lock step so that the channel couples them.

1.2 Reconstruction of relay B's inputs

  • Voltages: Sub B bus VT, i.e. VA/VB/VC of the SUBB L2-21 record (one-line note: both Sub B line relays take the same bus VT).
  • Currents: L1 has negligible charging, both ends have CTR = 240 and P1 toward the bus, so the true L1 secondary current at B is −IA (relay A record). Relay B measures −m·IA, where m is the share of the CT secondary current passing through the relay. As built (parallel BF-50): m = 0.06/(0.04 + 0.06) = 0.60 (resistive burdens, no phase shift). By design (series): m = 1.00.
  • Check of the basis: the L1 series impedance computed from relay A's currents and the two bus voltages, (VA − VB)/IA on the BC loop, is 3.95 + j38.46 Ω primary versus line data 4.00 + j38.40 Ω. So relay A's record and the Sub B voltages are mutually consistent, and no current left L1 between A and B (no L1 fault).

6. Item 3 – root cause and evidence

6.1 Why relay A tripped

Relay A behaved exactly as set. Its zone 2 (5.79 Ω sec = 125 % of L1) overreaches into L2 by design and asserted at k = 351 (Z = 1.33 + j5.50 Ω sec at pickup). It received a permissive at k = 406 and tripped by POTT. The permissive was relay B's echo, so relay A's trip is a consequence and not a cause.

6.2 Why relay B echoed

For a fault behind Substation B, relay B must assert Z3R (reverse) and block the echo. With the as-built CT circuit, relay B's BC-loop impedance during the fault averaged −1.225 − j1.124 Ω sec (|Z| = 1.66 Ω at −137.5°). It is 1.163 Ω from the Z3R circle centre, against a radius of 0.870 Ω, so it lies outside the circle. Z3R never asserted, EBLK never started, the AND gate was satisfied from RX (k = 363), and ECHO followed at k = 394. With the true current (m = 1) the same impedance is −0.735 − j0.674 Ω sec, 0.672 Ω from the centre, inside Z3R. The angle (−137.5°, away from the 264° Z3R axis) comes from the 3.0 Ω fault resistance seen through the 2.06 infeed ratio. The 1/0.60 magnification is what pushes the point out of the circle. 6.3 Documentary evidence of the parallel connection

  • Cable and termination schedule, Sub B bay L1, as built, rev C 2025-02-12 ("BF-50 replaced"): rows 3/4, 9/10 and 15/16 feed relay L1-21 (Z01/Z03/Z05) and BF50-L1 (A1/B1/C1) from the same test-switch terminals TS-L1:2/4/6. Rows 5/6, 11/12 and 17/18 return both relay outputs (Z02/Z04/Z06) and BF-50 outputs (A2/B2/C2) to the star point TB-L1:10. That is a parallel connection.
  • AC schematic B-E-3301 rev A: "all devices on core 1 are connected in SERIES in each phase (CT X2 → TS → device 1 in/out → device 2 in/out → N)". The bay L2 schedule (rev B 2023) is correct: L2-21:Z02 → BF50-L2:A1, BF50-L2:A2 → TB-L2:10.
  • Device technical data: L1-21 burden 0.04 Ω and BF-50 burden 0.06 Ω, both resistive, so the relay's share is 0.06/0.10 = 0.60 with no phase shift.
  • Maintenance log: the BF-50 replacement on 2025-02-12 was followed only by a trip-output test (no CT circuit re-commissioning). The CT commissioning book for Sub B dates from 2023-04-14, before the change. The 2025-06-03 relay B test injected at the relay test switch "with BF-50 isolated by its own test switch". Isolating the BF-50 removes the parallel path, so the test could not reveal the error. The error had no effect on security until ECHO = Y was introduced at B with R5 on 2026-05-26.

6.4 Consequences beyond this event

With m = 0.60 every reach of relay B is effectively multiplied by 0.60: Z1 covers 48 % of L1 instead of 80 %, Z2 75 % instead of 125 % (no longer overreaching, so POTT keying from B fails for faults near A), and Z3R covers 0.90 times instead of 1.50 times relay A's zone 2 overreach beyond bus B. The BF-50 receives only 40 % of the current, so its 50BF pickup is effectively 2.5 times its setting.

9. Item 5 – corrective action

  1. Immediate setting change at relay B (interim): ECHO = N (all other R5 values unchanged) until the CT circuit is corrected. The replay with ECHO = N and the as-built circuit gives no trip at A for this event. Relay B's dependability remains reduced (effective Z2 is 75 % of L1), so the rewiring must not wait.
  2. As-found confirmation before the change: record on load the relay B and BF-50 phase currents and compare them with relay A. Expected as found: relay B at 0.60 times relay A's magnitude and 180° opposite, and BF-50 at 0.40 times.
  3. Rewire to the design (B-E-3301): L1-21:Z02/Z04/Z06 → BF50-L1:A1/B1/C1 and BF50-L1:A2/B2/C2 → TB-L1:10. Remove TS-L1:2/4/6 → BF50-L1:A1/B1/C1 and L1-21:Z02/Z04/Z06 → TB-L1:10. Issue cable schedule rev D.
  4. Re-commission the CT 52-L1 core 1 circuit (form P-14): primary injection, or secondary injection at the CT terminals through the complete chain with all devices in circuit, followed by ratio, polarity and loop-resistance checks and an on-load check. Relay B must equal relay A within 1–2 % at 180°, and BF-50 must equal relay B. Repeat the POTT/echo end-to-end test, including a reverse-fault case that verifies Z3R blocking of the echo.
  5. Final settings at relay B after re-commissioning: restore ECHO = Y with R5 unchanged: Z1P 3.71, Z2P 5.79, Z3P 1.74 Ω sec, Z1ANG 84°, 50PP 0.50 A, EDPU 32, EDUR 64, EBLK 64 samples. The replay shows that these values coordinate for this event when the inputs are correct. Do not increase Z3P to compensate for the wiring: a reach above 2.22 Ω would only mask the error. Relay A: no change.
  6. Process: require CT circuit re-commissioning after any device change in a CT circuit. Do not test series-connected devices with other devices isolated without a follow-up through-current test. Apply this to the planned 2027 BF-50 replacement in bay L2 and audit other BF-50 retrofits for the same error.

10. Limitations

  • Relay B's oscillography was not available, so its inputs are reconstructed. The event data constrain relay B's current share to m ≤ 0.73. The value m = 0.60 comes from the documented parallel connection and the device burdens; lead resistance between the split point and the devices was neglected. The as-found on-load measurement (action 2) will confirm m.
  • Counterfactual analog data after k = 501 are a steady-state continuation; the fault had been cleared at k = 446.
