HOLLOWAY ROAD EXTENSION - STEEL FRAME C - ANALYSIS DATA SHEET (sheet 1 of 5)

Structure. The building has three parallel plane frames on frame lines B, C and D (sheets 3 and 4), each of three bays (grid lines 1 to 4) and four storeys (levels G, L1, L2, L3 and roof R). Only frame C is analysed. Floors and roof span one way between the frame lines, so frame C carries the floor and roof loads of a strip extending from frame line C half-way to frame line B and half-way to frame line D (the frame C load strip). Ties on grid lines 1 to 4 carry no floor load and are not part of the frame C analysis.

Drawings. Sheet 3: framing plans of L1 and L2. Sheet 4: framing plans of L3 and roof. Sheet 5: column schedule, bracing elevations of lines C and D with storey heights, and the wind on frame C. On a framing plan a beam label is written directly above the beam it describes. Column sections, splice positions and base types are given in the column schedule; a column mark is frame line / grid line (C/2 is the column on frame line C at grid 2).

Analysis model.
1. Linear-elastic, first-order plane frame analysis of frame C in the state at the time stated in the site log (sheet 2). One load case: the loads below, with no load factors.
2. Centreline model: columns on their grid lines, beams on their level lines; bay widths from the framing plans, storey heights from sheet 5. Joint size and connection eccentricity are ignored.
3. E = 210 GPa for every member. Axial and bending deformation are included; shear deformation is ignored. Bending is about the major axis of every beam and column.
4. Columns are continuous through every floor. Each column has one splice, at the height given in the column schedule. The section changes at the splice; the splice itself is a full-strength rigid joint.
5. Beam-to-column connections are rigid (moment-resisting) unless shown as simple on the framing plan. A simple connection transmits no moment: that beam end is pinned to the column, and the column stays continuous.
6. Column bases are fixed or pinned as given in the column schedule.
7. Braces are pin-ended and carry axial force only. Brace self-weight is ignored.

Loads on frame C.
1. Self-weight of every beam and column of frame C: mass x 9.81 N/kg, a uniform load along the member (vertical on beams, along the axis of columns).
2. Floor and roof area loads (kN/m2, given on each framing plan) act on the full span of every frame C beam at that level: line load = area load x width of the frame C load strip.
3. A storage zone adds its area load x width of the frame C load strip as a uniform line load over the zone length only, positioned by the dimensions on the framing plan.
4. Plant standing on a frame C beam is a vertical point load on that beam at the position the plant occupies, located by the dimensions on the roof plan.
5. Wind (sheet 5) is a set of horizontal point loads at the grid 1 joints of frame C, acting towards grid 4.
6. Temporary props are included only while they are installed.

Reporting conventions.
a. Horizontal displacement of a joint: in mm, positive towards grid 4.
b. Base reaction: vertical, in kN, upwards positive.
c. Beam end moment: the hogging moment at the beam end on the column centreline, in kNm.
d. Beam maximum sagging moment: the largest sagging moment anywhere along that beam, in kNm.
e. Column moment: the bending moment at the stated position on the column centreline, in kNm, positive when the face on the grid 4 side of the column (the side away from grid 1) is in tension.
f. Brace axial force: in kN, tension positive.
